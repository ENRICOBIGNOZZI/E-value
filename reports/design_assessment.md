# Design assessment

This is a research diagnosis, not a changed protocol. The frozen baseline remains
unchanged. No empirical data enter the diagnosis.

The public uniform moment certificate is **nu² = 0.01321480686738281** for the
main defaults. The design bet in a new comparison is delta0/(2nu²), with
delta0=0.0001. Scaling the score and risk bound consistently leaves the
e-process exactly unchanged; changing decimal returns into basis points cannot
repair its power.

For a *hypothetical constant conditional gain* of 0.0001 daily utility, the T2a
80% power certificate at individual alpha=5% requires about 24.34 million
observations. For a gain of 0.0002, it requires about 6.09 million. These are
**sufficient horizons from a conservative lower bound**, not necessary sample
sizes or a proof that the actual process cannot cross earlier. The dynamic
financial simulation does not satisfy the constant-mean plug-in assumptions.

The pilot must therefore measure actual evidence, actual inactivity and
opportunity cost. It must not replace the public bound with a sample variance,
choose favorable seeds or infer superiority from valid individual testing.

A second obstacle is logical rather than numerical. With 200 deterministic
scores +0.5 followed by 52 scores -0.5, nu²=1 and first-block lambda=0.1,
current annual log evidence is 6.13703 (>log20), and the historical mean is
positive although the current conditional gain is negative. This does not
contradict T1: the episode-wide null is false. It disproves an interpretation
of rejection as certification of current profitability.

The manuscript also states a prospective impossibility result for a law class
closed under replacing the next score by zero without changing the past.
Any past-measurable decision with uniform type-I control for nonpositive
next-period gain then has power at most alpha under every alternative: pair
each alternative with a null having exactly the same observed history.
This closure deliberately permits unrestricted nonstationarity and need not
hold in the persistent simulation DGP. It clarifies the need for temporal
assumptions; it is not claimed as a novel general theorem.

The one-change-per-year calendar further limits growth to 14 changes, including
a terminal decision that has no subsequent investment observations. Structural
recall is mechanically capped and cannot be the sole success criterion.

Before a larger final study can support a strong paper, the most useful research
questions are: whether a genuinely certified observable comparison-specific
moment envelope can be materially tighter; what inferential target can express
current rather than historical economic relevance; and how to assess the cost
of the annual decision bottleneck. These are potential new designs requiring
explicit assumptions, proofs and fresh evaluation. They are not silently
substituted for the supplied baseline.
